VoiceGuard

Is the person on this call really your customer?

What is this?

Software can now copy anyone's voice. VoiceGuard tells you whether a caller is real.

Fraudsters use free tools to clone a customer's voice from a few seconds of audio, then phone the bank pretending to be them. VoiceGuard listens to the call while it happens and tells the person who answered, in plain words, whether to trust the caller.

  2. 2It listens to the callWhile the caller talks, it checks the voice and the sound for signs of a fake.
  3. 3It gives a clear answerReal customer, double-check or stop, with the reasons.

    Decision support, not a gatekeeper. VoiceGuard helps a person decide; it never blocks a call on its own. In tests on simulated and recorded calls it reached the right verdict about nine times in ten, but real phone networks, unusual voices and new cloning tools can still fool it. Treat "Looks like the real customer" as no red flags found, not as proof. Only measurements, never audio, are kept in the activity record.
    
  
    

    


  
    
    
    How VoiceGuard works
    
    
    When someone phones in, VoiceGuard answers one question: is this the registered customer, or someone (or something) imitating them? It checks four things and turns them into one of three answers.
    

    
    The three answers
    
    
    Looks like the real customer. No red flags. Carry on as normal.
    
    
    Double-check this caller. Something doesn't add up. Ask a security question only the real customer could answer.
    
    
    Stop. Either the voice looks fake (cloned or computer-generated), or it is not the customer on file. Pause any payment or change, end the call and phone the customer back on their registered number.
    

    
    The four checks
    
    
      
      
    • Does the voice match the registered customer? The caller's voice is compared with the one on file.
      • 
      
    • Are there signs of a computer-generated voice? Voice-cloning and text-to-speech tools leave faint marks that this check looks for. It cannot see every tool, which is why the voice match matters too.
      • 
      
    • Is anything about the call itself unusual? An unknown number or a large payment makes VoiceGuard more careful.
      • 
      
    • Is the audio clear? Noise, echo or distortion make a call harder to judge. VoiceGuard allows for them, and tells you when it had to.
      • 
    
    

    
    What is inside
    
    
      
      
    • A voice detector that listens for the faint marks computer-generated speech leaves.
      • 
      
    • A voice matcher that compares the caller with the customer's voice on file.
      • 
      
    • A decision step that combines everything and allows for noise, echo and phone-line distortion, so a bad line is not mistaken for a fake.
      • 
      
    • A notebook that records each decision (the scores and the reasons, never any audio) so it can be reviewed later.
      • 
    
    

    
    Other words you will see
    
    
      
      
    • Risk level: one number for the whole picture. It is a relative score, not the chance the caller is fake. The bar shows where it turns from genuine to double-check to stop for this kind of call.
      • 
      
    • Early reading: VoiceGuard has heard only a few seconds. It can ask you to double-check, but will not tell you to stop until it is sure.
      • 
      
    • Listening: not enough clear speech yet, such as silence, a muted line or just noise. VoiceGuard says so instead of guessing.
      • 
    
    

    
    What it cannot do
    
    
    It will not catch every fake. A very good clone that sounds like the customer and is played down a clean line can pass, and an unusual real voice can be questioned. That is why it advises a person rather than deciding.
