Real-time detection of AI voice-cloning fraud on phone calls

Is the person on this call really your customer? A privacy-preserving decision layer, with numbers you can check.

1Problem statement

A voice no longer proves who is speaking.

A few seconds of speech are enough for free AI tools to copy someone's voice. Fraudsters phone banks as the customer and push through payments and account changes. The agent has seconds to decide.

!Why one detector is not enough
36%

error on real-world audio from the best published detector we tested. On its own benchmark it reports about 1%.

A current text-to-speech voice was flagged only 8% of the time.

Error rate (EER) · lower is better
2Objectives
  1. Detect cloned and computer-generated voices on live calls, in real time.
  2. Combine four independent kinds of evidence instead of trusting one detector.
  3. Say plainly how sure it is, and keep a person in charge.
  4. Measure honestly and keep callers' audio private.

5a · A fair test
13–15%

error from silence alone in our first pipeline: a benchmark shortcut, now guarded by a permanent test.

Silent parts only: a model that listens to the voice scores about 50%. Real-world audio is never used for training or thresholds.

5b · Combine the evidence
90%

balanced accuracy on speakers the system never saw, against 74% for the detector alone.

Genuine callers accepted and attacks flagged, averaged: 19 situations, 4,157 reads.

5c · Awkward situations
86.6%

in 14 situations it had never met: rooms, echo, clipping, hum, music, phone codecs, packet loss.

Weakest: phone + crowd noise 78%, clipped microphone 81%, phone + echoey room 83%, speakerphone 85%.

5d · Newer cloning tools
72%

of clean-line clones from a free open-source tool were flagged; 87–99% on phone or noisy lines.

Share of clones flagged. The detector alone sees 2–3% of kNN-VC and SpeechT5; the voice match does the rest.

6Conclusion

Stopping voice-clone fraud takes several partly independent signals, measured fairly and explained in plain words, not one clever detector. VoiceGuard reaches about 90% balanced accuracy on unseen speakers and 86.6% in situations it had never met, flags 72–100% of modern clones, and stores no audio. It is decision support, not a gatekeeper. When a real voice note was called fake, we fixed our own model and reported the cost.

!Honest limits
  • On real-world audio the detector alone is weak (36% error): it supports, it does not decide.
  • A clone that sounds exactly like the customer can still pass. Tested on simulated lines and codecs, not recorded real calls.
  • Indian-language results are a small synthetic pilot; alerts are simulated; authentication and TLS are not built.
What is next
  • Retrain on modern clones and real codec audio.
  • Consented real calls and Indian-language speech.
  • Real alerts, security hardening, edge inference.
