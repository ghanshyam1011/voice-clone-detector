# VoiceGuard: poster content pack

Everything you need to put on the poster, written to be pasted into PowerPoint, Canva or Word.
Every number is from this repository's own results (`results/tables/`, `docs/`); where a figure
comes from a table, the file is named so a judge can check it. Seven ready-made figures are in
[`docs/poster_figures/`](poster_figures/) (300 dpi PNG, regenerated by
`python scripts/make_poster_figures.py`).

**The finished poster is built from this content:** [`poster/voiceguard_poster_A1.pdf`](poster/voiceguard_poster_A1.pdf)
(A1 portrait, vector, prints cleanly at A0-A3; a PNG and the editable HTML sit beside it). The copy is tightened from
the text below to fit, the charts are redrawn at poster size from the same tables, and the three console screens are real
runs of the built-in demo clips. It follows the college checklist: title, group members with roll numbers, subject and
faculty, problem statement, objectives, methodology / workflow diagram, technologies and tools used, results, conclusion,
and two signature boxes (faculty, external examiner) at the bottom. Regenerate it with

```
python scripts/make_poster.py --subject "Scalable ML and BDA" --faculty "Prof. Shweta Jaiswar" \
    --college "Vidyalankar Institute of Technology" --term "BE CMPN, Sem 7 · AY 2025-26" \
    --members "Ghanshyam Kumavat, 23102A0043"
python scripts/capture_console_shots.py     # only to refresh the console screens (serve_demo.py must be running)
```

The details are saved in `docs/poster/team.json`, so a plain `python scripts/make_poster.py` keeps them; pass a flag to
change one. Several members: `--members "Name, Roll; Name, Roll"`. A field you have not given shows a yellow placeholder.
The QR code points at the repository; it was checked by decoding the printed PDF, but scan it once with a phone
before printing, and make sure the repository is public.

**Rule for a poster:** someone should understand it from two metres away in under a minute.
Keep the copy below short, make the figures big, and leave the detail for your viva
([`viva_notes.md`](viva_notes.md)).

## 2. The copy (paste these, box by box)

### 1. The problem *(55 words)*

> A few seconds of someone's speech is now enough for free AI tools to copy their voice.
> Fraudsters use cloned voices to phone banks and call centres, pretend to be a customer, and push
> through payments or account changes. A familiar voice can no longer prove who is speaking, and
> the agent has seconds to decide.

### 2. Why detectors are not enough *(60 words)* + **Figure 1** (`1_honest_gap.png`)

> Published detectors report about **1 %** error on the benchmark they were built for. On real-world
> audio the best one we tested errs on about **1 call in 3 (36 %)**, and a modern text-to-speech
> voice slipped past it 92 % of the time. Betting a bank's decision on one detector fails silently
> the first time it meets a cloning tool it has not seen.

*Caption for Figure 1:* Equal error rate (lower is better). Same detector, three kinds of audio. Source:
`results/tables/cm_*` and the README results table.

### Our idea *(35 words, put it in a coloured box)*

> **Do not trust one detector.** Combine four partly independent kinds of evidence, say plainly how
> sure we are, and keep a person in charge of the decision.

### 3. How it works *(110 words)* + **Figure 3** (`3_architecture.png`), the largest picture on the poster

> 1. **Listen and clean.** An input gate repairs bad audio and says *WAITING* when there is nothing to
>    judge. A fair front-end treats real and fake audio identically.
> 2. **Gather four kinds of evidence.** A neural **voice detector** (AASIST), a **voice match** against
>    the customer's voice on file (WavLM), the **recording style** (noise, echo, clipping, which tells
>    us how far to trust the detector on this line) and the **call details** (known number? amount?).
> 3. **Combine and decide.** A learned fusion and a policy turn the evidence into **ALLOW, VERIFY or
>    ESCALATE** with plain-English reasons, stricter for a payment than for a routine question.
> 4. **Act and record.** The operator console, REST and gRPC all use one engine. The audit log keeps
>    scores and reasons, **never audio**.

### 4. What the operator sees *(30 words)* + **2-3 screenshots of the console** (see section 4 below)

> One answer in plain words, the four checks behind it, and what to do now. A scorecard says
> whether the system was right, including when it missed.

### 5. Can you trust the numbers? *(75 words)* + **Figure 2** (`2_silence_shortcut.png`)

> On the standard benchmark a model can score well by listening to **the silent parts of the clips**
> instead of the voice: our first pipeline scored **13-15 % error on silence alone** (guessing is
> 50 %). We built a fair front-end and a **permanent silence test**; every number on this poster
> passes it. Real-world audio is never used for training or for choosing thresholds, and every result
> carries its provenance.

*Caption:* A model that is really listening to the voice should know nothing from silence (about 50 %).

### 6. Results *(90 words)* + **Figures 4, 5, 6**

> - **Combining evidence beats any single signal:** 74 % (detector alone) → 90 % balanced accuracy on
>   speakers the system never saw. *(Figure 4)*
> - **Rooms, echo, clipping, phone codecs, hum, music, packet loss:** in 14 situations it had never
>   met, balanced accuracy rose from 78.6 % to 86.6 %; 91.8 % on 10 untouched speakers across 19
>   situations. *(Figure 5)*
> - **Newer cloning tools:** the detector alone sees only 2-3 % of them; the voice match carries the
>   rest. 72 % of clean-line open-source clones were flagged, 83-99 % on noisy or phone lines, about half
>   of laptop-class clones, all of synthetic speech. *(Figure 6)*

### 7. A lesson from real audio *(60 words)* + **Figure 7** (`7_why_a_real_voice_note_was_flagged.png`)

> A real WhatsApp voice note was called fake. The cause was inside our own model: it had learned the
> *recording style* of the training data. We changed it so recording style can only change **how much
> the detector is believed**, never raise an alarm alone. Cost: 1-3 points of benchmark accuracy,
> reported openly.

### Honest limits *(70 words, a clearly marked box; judges respect this)*

> - On real-world audio the detector alone is weak (36 % error): it supports, it does not decide.
> - A clone that sounds exactly like the customer can still pass.
> - Tested on simulated rooms, lines and codecs, not recorded real calls; the Indian-language results
>   are a small synthetic pilot.
> - Alerts are simulated; authentication and TLS are not built.

### What's next *(40 words)*

> Retrain the detector on modern cloning tools and real-world codec audio; collect consented real
> calls and Indian-language speech; real alert delivery; security hardening; edge inference.

### References and thanks *(small print)*

AASIST (Jung et al., ICASSP 2022) · ASVspoof 2019 (Wang et al.) · In-the-Wild (Müller et al.,
Interspeech 2022) · WavLM (Chen et al., 2022) · RawBoost (Tak et al., ICASSP 2022) · kNN-VC (Baas et
al., 2023) · MMS-TTS (Pratap et al., 2023) · Silero VAD · LibriSpeech. Datasets and models belong to
their authors and are used under their own licences.

## 4. Screenshots to take from the live console (done for the finished poster by `scripts/capture_console_shots.py`; do this yourself only for a different layout)

Open the console (`python scripts/serve_demo.py`, then `http://127.0.0.1:8000`), set the browser
window to about 1440 × 900, zoom 100 %, light theme, and use the Windows Snipping Tool (Win+Shift+S).
Take these three, then crop tight and add a thin grey border:

1. **A real customer** (play *The real customer calls*): the green *Looks like the real customer* answer,
   the meter near zero, the four green checks, the *Correct* scorecard.
2. **A fake voice** (play *Another cloned voice* or *A computer-generated voice*): the red *Stop*
   answer, the meter in the red zone, the red checks, the *Caught* scorecard.
3. **The honest hard case** (play *A voice cloned with a free tool*): an early warning that eases off, the
   *Partly caught* scorecard. This one earns trust: a poster that shows a miss is believed on its hits.

Caption idea: *"The operator sees one plain-language answer and the reasons. The scorecard tells the truth
even when we miss."*

Backup: record a 60-second screen video of the same three calls and put a QR code to it on the poster.

## 5. If you want an extra box (pick one, only if there is room)

**Privacy by design** *(45 words)*: *"Audio is processed in memory and never stored. The audit log keeps
only scores, reasons, model version and time, and refuses any field that could carry audio, a transcript
or a voiceprint. We do not claim legal compliance; that needs a legal review."*

**Indian languages** *(45 words)*: *"A small synthetic pilot in Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Bengali and English
shows the detector's hit rate varies a lot by language (8 % to 85 %). Per-language support has to be
measured, not assumed; a consented real-speech set is the next step."*

**Built to be integrated** *(35 words)*: *"One decision engine over REST, WebSocket and gRPC, tested to give
identical answers on the same clip. Calls are re-scored every half second; a confirmed read needs about
4 s of speech."*

## 6. What to say at the poster (so you are not reading it out)

**30 seconds.** "Free AI tools can now copy a customer's voice, and fraudsters use it to call banks. We built
VoiceGuard, which listens to a live call and tells the agent in plain words whether to trust the caller: real
customer, double-check, or stop. It does not rely on one detector. It combines the detector, a match against
the customer's voice, the recording quality and the call details, and a person makes the final call."

**90 seconds.** Add: "The most important thing we did is measure honestly. The standard benchmark has a
hidden shortcut, so we built a fair test, and a detector that looks nearly perfect in the lab (about 1 % error)
turned out to err on about one call in three on real-world audio. So we combine evidence instead. We tested it in 19 awkward
situations, rooms, echo, phone lines, clipping, and published where it fails, and we even show a cloned
voice that gets through. When a real WhatsApp voice note was flagged as fake, we traced the cause to a
shortcut inside our own model and fixed it, and we report what the fix cost."

**If someone asks "is it ready to deploy?"** "Not yet, and we say why: the detector is weak on real-world audio,
we have tested simulated rather than recorded calls, and there is no authentication layer. What is ready is the
method, the engine and the honest measurements."

## 7. Do not put on the poster

- "100 % accurate", "detects all deepfakes", "works for all Indian languages", "DPDP compliant" or
  "prevents fraud". None is supported by evidence in this project.
- The word **"fake"** for a person who simply is not the registered customer. The console says
  *"isn't the customer on file"* for that case; use the same care on the poster.
- Dense tables and code. Put one big number and one figure per idea.
- Numbers from the older linear clone head (100 % kNN-VC catch, 92.7 % dev balanced accuracy). They were partly
  an artefact of recording style; the shipped figures are the ones above.
- Anything you cannot explain in the viva. If a figure is on the poster, you should be able to say what the axes
  are and where the data came from.
